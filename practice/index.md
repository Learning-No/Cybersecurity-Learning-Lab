<section class="hub-hero"><p class="eyebrow">SMALL CONSISTENT PRACTICE</p><h1>Build skill by <em>making things.</em></h1><p class="lead">You do not need expensive subscriptions to begin. Use local machines, intentionally vulnerable labs, sample logs, documentation, and small write-ups. Keep every exercise in scope.</p></section>
<section class="section"><div class="hub-grid">
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">15 MINUTES</p><h2>Concept of the day</h2><ol><li>Read one official documentation page.</li><li>Explain the concept in 3–5 sentences.</li><li>Draw a small diagram.</li><li>Write one misconception and correct it.</li></ol></article>
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">30 MINUTES</p><h2>Observe your own system</h2><ol><li>Inspect local network settings or security configuration.</li><li>Record a baseline without changing settings.</li><li>Explain what each observation means.</li><li>Redact personal details from your notes.</li></ol></article>
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">45 MINUTES</p><h2>Guided lab session</h2><ol><li>Read the lab rules and allowed scope.</li><li>Follow the exercise's intended steps.</li><li>Capture only the evidence needed.</li><li>Write impact, fix, and lessons learned.</li></ol></article>
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">WEEKLY</p><h2>Build a mini-project</h2><p>Choose one: DNS lookup worksheet, Linux baseline, Windows security checklist, fictional threat model, sample-log timeline, or sanitized lab report.</p><p><b>Definition of done:</b> someone else can understand your question, method, evidence, conclusion, and limitations.</p></article>
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">SAFE PRACTICE</p><h2>Free training platforms</h2><p>Use platforms that clearly identify their authorized learning environment. Read rules before interacting with a target.</p><ul><li><a href="https://portswigger.net/web-security" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PortSwigger Web Security Academy</a></li><li><a href="https://owasp.org/www-project-juice-shop/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OWASP Juice Shop</a> (run locally)</li><li><a href="https://picoctf.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">picoCTF</a></li><li><a href="https://overthewire.org/wargames/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OverTheWire</a></li></ul></article>
<article class="hub-card"><p class="tag">REFLECTION</p><h2>Keep a learning log</h2><p>For each session, answer: What question did I investigate? What did I expect? What did I observe? What are the limitations? What would I do next? What must remain private?</p></article>
</div></section>
<section class="ethics"><div><p class="eyebrow">STOP CONDITION</p><h2>If permission or scope is unclear, stop.</h2><p>Do not practice on random public IPs, school or workplace networks, or someone else's account. Use local labs, sample datasets, and explicitly authorized platforms.</p></div></section>
