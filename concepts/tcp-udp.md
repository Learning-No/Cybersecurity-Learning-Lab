<article class="learning-page"><a class="back-link" href="{{ '/concepts/' | relative_url }}">← Concepts library</a><p class="eyebrow">CONCEPT 03 / PROTOCOLS</p><h1>TCP vs UDP: <em>two transport approaches</em></h1><p class="lead">TCP provides a connection-oriented byte stream with sequencing, acknowledgements, and retransmission. UDP sends independent datagrams without TCP's delivery and ordering guarantees.</p>
<div class="diagram-panel"><div class="protocol-columns"><div><h3>TCP · reliability features</h3><div class="packet-row">Sender <span>→ SYN</span></div><div class="packet-row">Receiver <span>← SYN-ACK</span></div><div class="packet-row">Sender <span>→ ACK</span></div><div class="packet-row">Data <span>→ ordered stream</span></div><p class="diagram-caption">Acknowledgements and retransmission can help recover lost data. They add protocol overhead and do not guarantee an application is secure.</p></div><div><h3>UDP · datagrams</h3><div class="packet-row">Sender <span>→ Packet 1</span></div><div class="packet-row">Sender <span>→ Packet 2</span></div><div class="packet-row">Sender <span>→ Packet 3</span></div><div class="packet-row">Receiver <span>may see gaps</span></div><p class="diagram-caption">UDP keeps the transport mechanism lightweight; an application can add its own reliability where needed.</p></div></div></div>
<h2>Comparison</h2><div class="comparison-table"><div class="comparison-head"><b>Property</b><b>TCP</b><b>UDP</b></div><div><span>Connection</span><span>Connection-oriented</span><span>Connectionless datagrams</span></div><div><span>Ordering</span><span>Ordered byte stream</span><span>No general ordering guarantee</span></div><div><span>Delivery recovery</span><span>ACK/retransmission</span><span>Handled by application if needed</span></div><div><span>Typical examples</span><span>HTTPS, SSH, email</span><span>DNS commonly, voice/video, gaming</span></div></div>
<h2>How to reason about it</h2><ol class="process-list"><li>Identify the application and its latency/reliability needs.</li><li>Determine whether the application uses TCP, UDP, or another transport.</li><li>Remember that a port identifies an endpoint for a transport protocol; TCP port 53 and UDP port 53 are distinct.</li><li>Do not infer a vulnerability from a protocol or port number alone—confirm context, exposure, configuration, and authorization.</li></ol>
<h2>Safe practice</h2><p>Read protocol metadata in a sample capture or capture your own browser traffic with Wireshark. Avoid capturing other people's traffic; traces may expose DNS queries, device details, and other private information.</p><p class="resource-links"><a href="https://www.wireshark.org/docs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wireshark documentation ↗</a><a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TCP specification ↗</a><a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc768" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UDP specification ↗</a></p></article>
